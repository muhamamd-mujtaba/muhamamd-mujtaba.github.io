## A Turning Point After My Mid Exams

The mid exams of my second semester became a turning point in my academic journey. Before the exams, I was mainly focused on completing the syllabus and preparing for different subjects within a limited time. Like many students, I was under pressure because several classes had been conducted online, and I felt that my preparation was not as strong as it should have been. Although I worked hard during the mid week, the results were not exactly what I had expected. Instead of becoming discouraged, I decided to reflect on my experience and identify where I needed improvement.

One subject that completely changed my mindset after the mids was Database Systems. Initially, I considered it another technical course that I simply had to pass. However, after spending more time understanding its concepts, I realized that databases are much more than just tables and SQL queries. They are the foundation of almost every modern software application. This realization increased my curiosity and encouraged me to study the subject more seriously.

## Discovering the Importance of Databases

After the mids, I started revising database topics one by one instead of studying only for exams. I realized that every concept was connected with another concept. Learning about tables naturally led me to understand primary keys, foreign keys, relationships, normalization, and SQL queries. Instead of memorizing definitions, I began understanding the purpose behind each concept.

For example, when I learned about normalization, I understood why unnecessary data repetition creates problems and how proper database design improves efficiency. Concepts like First Normal Form, Second Normal Form, and Third Normal Form no longer seemed difficult because I started connecting them with real-life examples. Every topic made me realize that a well-designed database is the backbone of every successful information system.

This change in thinking made studying more enjoyable. Instead of learning only for marks, I started learning because I genuinely wanted to understand how databases work.

## Learning Through Dr. Bilal Ahmad's Teaching

One of the biggest reasons behind my growing interest in Database Systems was the teaching approach of Dr. Bilal Ahmad. His lectures focused on understanding concepts instead of simply memorizing them. Whenever a new topic was introduced, he explained why it was important in practical software development rather than limiting the discussion to theory.

This approach helped me understand that database knowledge is useful in many different areas of computer science. Whether someone wants to become a software engineer, web developer, data analyst, or machine learning engineer, understanding databases is an essential skill.

I also noticed that many programming projects require proper database design. Without a good database, even the best application cannot store or manage information effectively. This practical connection made the subject much more interesting for me.

## Building Confidence Through Practice

After my mids, I made a personal decision to practice database concepts regularly. Instead of reading notes only once, I started solving SQL queries and reviewing examples repeatedly. At first, writing queries was confusing because every statement had a specific syntax. However, the more I practiced, the easier it became.

I learned how to create tables, insert records, retrieve information, update data, and delete unnecessary records. Later, I became comfortable using aggregate functions such as COUNT(), SUM(), AVG(), MAX(), and MIN(). Writing SELECT queries also became more natural with continuous practice.

Another topic that increased my confidence was learning relationships between tables using primary keys and foreign keys. Initially, these concepts seemed difficult, but after practicing examples, I realized that they actually make databases more organized and efficient.

## Understanding the Value of Normalization

Among all the topics, normalization became one of my favorite concepts. Before studying it carefully, I believed that storing all information in one table would make things easier. However, I later understood that this approach creates duplicate data and causes maintenance problems.

Learning First Normal Form, Second Normal Form, and Third Normal Form completely changed my understanding of database design. I realized that normalization is not only an academic topic but also an important technique used in real-world systems. Proper normalization reduces redundancy, improves consistency, and makes databases easier to manage.

As I solved more examples, I started appreciating the logical thinking required for database design. It felt satisfying to organize information correctly and understand why each table should have a specific purpose.

## Connecting Database Knowledge with Future Technologies

Another reason my interest increased was realizing that databases are closely connected with modern technologies. During different discussions, I learned that artificial intelligence, machine learning, web development, mobile applications, and cloud computing all depend on well-managed data.

No matter how advanced an application becomes, it still needs an organized database to store, retrieve, and manage information efficiently. This understanding made me appreciate Database Systems even more because I could see how the subject supports many other areas of computer science.

It also motivated me to continue improving my SQL skills because I know they will remain useful throughout my academic and professional journey.

## A Better Study Routine

The mid exams also taught me an important lesson about preparation. I realized that understanding technical subjects requires consistent practice rather than last-minute study sessions. Database concepts become much easier when they are revised regularly.

After the mids, I tried to maintain a better study routine by reviewing lecture notes, practicing SQL queries, and solving exercises every week. This gradual approach reduced my stress and improved my understanding. Instead of studying only before exams, I started treating learning as a continuous process.

This habit also improved my confidence because every practice session added something new to my knowledge.

## Looking Ahead

Today, Database Systems has become one of the subjects I genuinely enjoy studying. What started as a challenging course gradually became one of my strongest academic interests. My mid exams did not only test my preparation; they also helped me discover a subject that I now appreciate much more deeply.

I understand that there is still a lot to learn, including advanced SQL, database optimization, indexing, transactions, stored procedures, and database security. However, I now feel motivated instead of overwhelmed. Every new topic gives me another opportunity to improve my technical skills.
